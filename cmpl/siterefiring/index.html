Test Case: CT-1021 — Sustained Third-Party Re-Firing Detection

Domain key: siterefiring (rogue vendor)

Purpose: Simulates a third-party vendor that honours a rejection only for as long as anyone is watching. It falls silent the moment consent is rejected, so a point-in-time check taken straight after the click reports a clean pass — and then it resumes calling home a little later, and keeps doing so on every page the visitor opens afterwards, with no new interaction to justify it.

Welcome to SiteReFiring

This page carries one badly behaved vendor. Rejecting cookies here does stop it — briefly. Watch the activity log below for a minute or two after making a choice.

Vendor behaviour on this page

Before any choice — beacons every 15 seconds, establishing the baseline the test measures against, plus a third-party video embed that sets cookies on the vendor's own domain.

At the moment of rejection — everything stops. Nothing fires for the next 40 seconds.

40 seconds later — beacons resume every 25 seconds and never stop again.

On every page load after that — quiet for 25 seconds, then beaconing resumes, so each navigation in the monitoring window repeats the pattern.

The beacons use Image() rather than injected script or iframe tags, so tag-level auto-blocking does not stop them.

Live vendor activity

Times are your local clock. The timeline restarts in every new tab; consent-ish cookies left on this domain by the other test sites are absorbed as the starting baseline, not mistaken for a choice made here.

Third-party embed

Loaded only while no consent decision is on file, so the scan inventory picks up non-essential cookies set on the vendor's own domain.

What the scanner expects to find here

  • Banner present, with a reject-all choice, so the reject-all state is reached
  • Post-rejection monitoring window runs: 3 navigations and at least 3 minutes
  • At least one third-party vendor domain active before rejection and again inside the window
  • Expected result: fail — sustained re-firing detected

Control case

The compliant variant is identical up to the moment of the decision — same vendors, same baseline — and then stops for good. Register it as its own domain and scan both to compare a pass against a fail.
